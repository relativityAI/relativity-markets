# Cycle Duration: Long, Medium and Short-Dated Cycles

Before investing in a sector, decide **how long its cycle is likely to run**. The same signal means different things depending on duration. A "peak" in a short cycle may be over in a year; a "bull phase" in a long cycle can run for most of a decade.

Contents: 1 Why it matters | 2 Long-dated | 3 Medium-dated | 4 Short-dated | 5 Classification checklist | 6 Exit criteria

---

## 1. Why it matters

- It sets the **holding horizon** and the **exit expectation**.
- Sector labels mislead: "power" is not automatically short-cycle. Sub-segments of the same broad sector can have different durations (e.g., within electrical equipment, some products face near-term oversupply while others have multi-year shortages).
- A rule of thumb from market folklore: **the longer the bear market, the longer the next bull market**.

---

## 2. Long-dated cycles (roughly 6-15 years per leg)

### Characteristics
- Driven by **structural change** and multi-year investment programmes.
- Preceded by a **long bear market that destroyed supply** (no new investment for years), including the arrival of "brand new" industries after supply was wiped out.
- Typical sectors: capital goods, power and grid equipment, large infrastructure build-outs, industries tied to national investment programmes.
- Bull and bear legs are both long, because the industry overbuilds, then underinvests for years, and then demand overtakes supply again.

### Examples (mechanism, from the source video)
- **Gas turbines (US):** a power-plant boom in roughly 2003-2008 led to heavy oversupply and then a 10-14 year downcycle; a supplier's profits were near zero or loss-making for years before recovering. When a trigger finally arrived, the next bull market was also long.
- **Transformers / grid:** about a decade of lull followed by a multi-year (roughly six-year) bull market.
- **Hotels:** about 14 years of lull (2008-2022 in the Indian example) followed by a strong run with growing profits.
- **HVDC and transmission:** very little investment for decades implied a decade-long catch-up need; **insulators and bushings** were cited as having multi-year supply-demand mismatches (4-6 years for insulators; 24-30 months for bushings).
- **Power overall:** the video's view was that the *eventual* bear market would only come once thermal and nuclear build-outs are complete and power becomes oversupplied; that is many years away.

### How to treat it
- Coming out of a very long bear market (for example 15 years), expect **deeper legs to run**.
- Still define exit rules; long does not mean forever. Within a long cycle, individual sub-segments can top much earlier (see the capital-cycle phases in `four-drivers.md`).

## 3. Medium-dated cycles (roughly 2-4 years)

### Characteristics
- Industries that are **structurally attractive but shallowly cyclical**: capital cycles evolve with the structure, so profits move in multi-year waves.
- In the end, **the company with more touch points and a better brand wins**; consolidation is the typical end state.
- Common sources of the "bust" end: private equity funding of new entrants, or incumbents all adding capacity in the same geography.

### Examples
- **Diagnostics:** strong run on the back of a demand shock; capital flooded in (private equity, large entrants such as e-pharmacies/platforms); prices, multiples and margins fell.
- **Hospitals:** if all hospital chains build greenfield capacity in the same cities, the end state is a **pricing war** (watch tier-1 cities).
- **Wealth managers / asset managers:** structural tailwind (financialisation) with shallower, repeating cycles.
- **CDMO / contract manufacturing:** earnings strong for the next 24-36 months in the example, but a valuation-led bubble can form once "everyone agrees it's the epic sector". Watch for it after roughly 8-10 further quarters of consensus.

### How to treat it
- Plan for 2-4 years; track whether new capital is entering and whether the market is starting to treat the sector as a consensus favourite.

## 4. Short-dated cycles (roughly 1-2 years, commodity-driven)

### Characteristics
- **Commodity price cycles**: one good year, followed by two or three bad years as supply catches up.
- Earnings and margins spike and then crash; stock returns follow.

### Examples
- **Graphite electrodes:** margins went from low single digits to over 60% at the peak, and a pair of companies' combined profit grew from tens of crores to thousands of crores in two years, then fell back for years.
- **Sugar:** one good year, two or three weak years.
- **Optical fibre:** a big cycle on the back of 5G capex in 2017-18, followed by oversupply from China. Note it is not a new sector: it has gone through multiple booms and busts. At one point global fibre output could wrap the earth several times.

### How to treat it
- Treat as a trade, not a thesis. Hold only while the commodity driver persists; expect sharp reversals.
- Check the commodity's own cycle and global supply response.

## 5. Classification checklist

For any sector, answer:

1. **How deep and how long was its last bear market?** (Longer → more likely a long next leg.)
2. **Is the driver structural (policy, technology, demographics) or commodity/price-driven?**
3. **What is the global history of this sector?** (Did the same industry boom and bust elsewhere? E.g., foreign solar manufacturers went bankrupt on oversupply; in India, oversupply ends in insolvency proceedings. Did a data-centre build-out run for several years abroad before it reached a market like the user's?)
4. **Has a structural shift extended the cycle** (a new demand source, regulation, technology)? If yes, the cycle may be longer than the usual pattern, but multiples may not match prior cycles.
5. **How is supply responding right now?** (See capital-cycle phases.)
6. **Which sub-segment is it?** Classify sub-segments individually; they can differ.

Output: Long / Medium / Short, with a one-line justification and the **expected remaining runway** as a range, flagged as an estimate.

---

## 6. Exit criteria (every sector holding needs one)

Because even attractive sectors eventually turn, define exit or de-risk triggers *before* investing. Candidates:

- **Rate of change** of revenue/earnings growth decelerating for several quarters in a row.
- **Margin slippage across the sector** (not just one company) for 3+ quarters.
- **New entrants and IPO waves** in the sector.
- **Valuation percentile** versus the sector's own history, especially approaching the top of its range while growth slows.
- **Stage-4 / lower-highs breakdown** on the sector's custom index.
- **Time-frame expiry**: the cycle has run roughly as long as its type suggests (short ~1-2 yr; medium ~2-4 yr; long ~6+ yr) and other drivers are turning.
- **Narrative saturation**: everyone agrees it is the epic sector.

Write the chosen triggers into the final report.
