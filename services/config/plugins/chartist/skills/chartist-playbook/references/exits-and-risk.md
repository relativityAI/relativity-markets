# Exits, Risk & Investor Psychology

The guest's one-line summary of every great trader's system: **"Risk management and probability." Lose less, win more, strong stocks in strong themes.** This file covers how to exit, the math that keeps you honest, and the biases that break people.

## The exit is decided before the entry

- Before taking any position, write down the exit: **thesis broken** (the catalyst dies) **or technical stop hit**. After that, **do not interfere with the system**. The advice given on the show, half-joking: "go travel, don't come to the screen."
- Why: the market's damage comes from your own emotions after entry. A pre-decided exit converts future panic into a past decision.

## Trailing stops — the answer to "how high can it go?"

- "How much do you love me?" has no quantifiable answer, and neither does "how much higher will it run?" Nobody can sell the exact top, and **giving back some of the peak gain is part of the system, not a failure** — the trailing stop buys you the timely exit that valuation-guessing never will.
- Case: the guest held a hardware exporter bought near ₹900; the crowd shouted "overvalued" at 3,000, 4,000, 5,000… it peaked at ~11,000 on a 100x+ P/E. The trailing stop — not valuation forecasting — captured the trend and forced the exit. For an *existing* holder a peak region is only an exit signal via the stop; for a *new* entrant that last 50% leg is a late-stage entry, a completely different trade.
- Mechanics: trail under weekly structure / the 30–40 week EMA. Tighten in stage 3. Never widen the stop "because the thesis still looks good" — that's the interference rule.

## Distribution endings determine exit style

Re-read `stage-analysis.md` §"Climactic tops vs slow distribution" — two endings:
- **Slow distribution:** the chart gives weeks/months of lower-highs warning. Time to exit with dignity; use it.
- **Climactic top:** vertical move + one-bar reversal on the first external shock (the 18-Oct-2021 broad-market example: IRCTC, DMart, IEX topped the same day). If your position is riding a climax, the stop *is* the exit plan — there is no orderly warning.

## The math that decides arguments (use this table constantly)

| Drawdown | Gain required to break even |
|---|---|
| −20% | +25% |
| −50% | +100% |
| −90% | +900% |

- **Losses work geometrically against you; risk works exponentially** (Minervini, *Think & Trade Like a Champion*, ~p.47: "risk and reward are not proportional").
- Percentages compound: a "5x" stock gained 400%, not 500% — so after a 50% correction it hasn't kept 350%, it's left ~150%. People do this math with feelings; do it with exponents.
- Corollary: averaging down in a falling stage-4 stock is buying a geometric problem with money.

## Position management rules

1. **Don't exit 100% because a new trend begins.** After a deep market correction, the survivors of the base are positioned for the next multi-bagger leg; small-cap/strong-theme leaders historically re-rate hardest off the lows. Partial trimming is management; wholesale abandonment of good structures is fear.
2. **Weekly-chart investors don't get shaken out by daily-chart volatility.** The Apar Industries shakeout: −20%+ in three weeks (1,800→1,400) *without* a true 40-week-MA break, then ~1,400→~10,000. If you'd sold on the daily chart's pain, you sold the multi-bagger to escape a shakeout. Smooth the ride: weekly charts, and accept that investors give back some top-side gain.
3. **Pullback headaches vs full moves:** every intraday dip will look like the end. If you must act on every pullback, you will never ride a complete move. Size positions so that no single shakeout is emotionally survivable — position size is the real risk-management tool (the podcast name for the whole game: probability + risk management).
4. **Averaging down is a stage question, not a price question.** Averaging into a stage-2 base that's testing the MA is a plan; averaging down into lower highs/lower lows is loss-aversion performing as strategy (the DHFL case below).

## The investor-bias taxonomy (Kahneman's framing)

From *Thinking Fast and Slow*: **cognitive biases** (information-processing errors — you *can* correct these by becoming aware) vs **belief biases** (identity-level — these change slowly). Everyone is biased, including professionals; the defense is awareness + systems, not purity.

| Bias | Signature in markets | Podcast case |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmation bias** | After researching a business, you can only hear good news; negative news makes you angry; you stop taking calls from the friend who disagrees | Solar-theme stage-4 calls got mobbed by commenters while companies themselves warned of overcapacity |
| **Conservatism bias** | First belief becomes permanent; refusing to update | "ITC is undervalued forever" — likes on the ITC-falling tweet came from holders who *couldn't* change their view |
| **Endowment bias** | Owned = worth more; opportunity cost invisible | HDFC Bank holder refusing to notice ~5 years of flat price; the DMart-2017 buyer defending 4–5 years of zero returns with "I bought it in 2017" |
| **Loss aversion / disposition effect** | Cut winners early, hold losers; "I'll sell at my cost price" | The break-even-price sellers create the overhead supply above every base |
| **Sunk-cost fallacy + anchoring** | Anchoring to cost price and time spent | **The DHFL uncle:** bought at 350–400, averaged down until average price 70–75, 70% of portfolio in one name, exited at 40–45. Mental toll >> the ₹ loss. Journal would have caught it |
| **Recency bias** | What happened last quarter feels like the regime | Tariff headlines and gold's run making everyone a precious-metals permabull while forgetting the last equity cycle; China un-loved until its index moved |
| **FOMO / "super-deprivation reaction" syndrome** | The dog whose bone is taken will bite; the Thar driver sees a yellow light and *will* jump it | Buying vertical moves because they're not in your portfolio |
| **Mental accounting** | "My 2021 money" vs "my trading money" — reframes losses as different buckets | Feeds the averaging-down loop |

**Practical defenses recommended in the podcast:**
1. **Journal every investment decision** (buy thesis, expected exit, what would change your mind) — the record is the mirror that biases cannot argue with.
2. Read *The Art of Thinking Clearly* (Rolf Dobelli) — two-page stories, one per bias, memorable enough to catch yourself in real time.
3. Accept that **you will still be wrong** after learning everything — analysis reduces probability, it doesn't guarantee outcomes; that's why risk management, not brilliance, is the cornerstone.
4. "How much do you love me?" — refuse to answer unanswerable questions with price targets; answer them with trailing stops.

## A short script for coaching a stuck user

When a user is holding a losing position with "fundamentals are fine":
1. Diagnose the stage honestly from the chart structure they describe (lower highs + falling MA = stage 4 regardless of fundamentals; good business ≠ good stock).
2. Run the recovery table on their actual drawdown.
3. Separate the two questions: *Is the thesis alive?* (catalyst check) vs *Is the timing alive?* (stage check). Both must be alive; either dead = exit logic.
4. If averaging down: ask whether it's a plan (stage-2 base, position sized) or a feeling (cost-price anchoring).
5. End with the interference rule: exit decided in advance, then execute without renegotiation.
