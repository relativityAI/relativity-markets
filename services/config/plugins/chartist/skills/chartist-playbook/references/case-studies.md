# Case Study Index (from the podcast)

Every case study mentioned in the SOIC × Himanshu Sharma podcast, indexed by the lesson it teaches. **All of these are recorded-episode teaching material with dates attached — not current calls.** If transcription made a name ambiguous, it's marked *(name as heard)*.

## Scanning & relative strength

| Case | Setup | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Lumax Auto Technologies** | Sat near its 52-week high for weeks (May–June window, ~500→1,500s territory) alongside peers SJS, Lumax Industries, Shri Ram Piston | The near-high scan hands you weeks of free study time; a cluster of names in one industry = theme signal; RS becomes the classic runner |
| **SJS Enterprises** | Auto-aesthetics name in the same near-high cluster; premiumization story (kit value per vehicle, decals + in-mold decoration divisions) | Same scan, second confirmation of the auto-ancillary theme; the pair with Lumax shows leader-vs-laggard within a theme |
| **Gold-loan NBFCs (CSB Bank, Fedbank Financial/FedFina, IIFL, Manappuram)** *(names as heard)* | All five hit the near-high scan in the same weeks around gold's move; each consolidated ~4 weeks under its July 52-week high (~125–130) | One scan → five names → common cause (gold) → common-sense theme read; RS vs a weak Nifty was visible on the chart itself |
| **Ashok Leyland** | ~12 weeks consolidating at 52-week highs (~130–140) before running (~120→185–190) | Market hands you 3 months to read a name before it moves; by the time "CV cycle started" became consensus, the scan had it early |
| **Kospi vs Nifty (memory chips)** | Kospi ~+90% in a year, Nifty sideways; SK Hynix/Samsung/Micron-theme = 40% of the index | Index-level RS read with the naked eye; theme (AI → edge inference → memory demand → +70–80% chip prices) + starting multiples at "industrial" P/Es (10–15x) = the classic top-down RS story |
| **Winners rotate every ~5 years (1991–present data)** | Real estate/infra → power/commodity (2008) → pharma dormancy 2013–19 → IT legs (Persistent ~55x, Alkyl Amines ~30x) → railway/defense → solar → oil → gold-loan | Don't marry last cycle's winners; after external shocks, watch which themes re-enter the scan first |

## Stage analysis & bases

| Case | Setup | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **BSE Ltd** | Down trend → sideways → ~1-year base → May-2021 volume-spike breakout above the 40-week EMA → multi-bagger; participated in both post-Covid cycles; IPO-based breakout nearby | Sleeping stock + volume spike = Darvas's "pay attention"; long base clears overhead supply; IPO-base breakouts get old — use the *relevant* high, not the listing-day one |
| **Garware Hi-Tech Films** | Multi-year base → breakout on the polyester-film → value-added (sun-control/BOPP protection) transition; management explicitly flagged margin expansion | Long base = assume business transition; read the concurrent investor presentation; ~5x leg |
| **Indigo (InterGlobe)** | 2023 long-base breakout into a near-virtual airline monopoly | Even large caps multi-bag off long bases; monopoly + long base = institutional entry |
| **HBL Engineering** | Long base → breakout with the Kavach (train collision avoidance) order catalyst visible in the same period's PPT | Long base + nameable catalyst = institutional endorsement; volume spikes confirm |
| **RVNL & Mazagon Dock** *(names as heard)* | RVNL: repeated 80–100% legs between long bases, cycle after cycle | Some names deliver the same structure repeatedly — know your repeat offenders |
| **Ashok Leyland (again)** | 2025 long-base breakout (July–September) after 12 weeks near highs; CV-finance/tire names (JK Tire, SML Isuzu, Tata Motors commercial) crowded the scan together | The scan is early theme detection — sector discovery from stock strength, never the reverse |
| **Aarti Drugs / Laurus Labs (~2017–2020 bases)** | Multi-year bases ending as EPS revisions turned positive | The estimate-revision → price-leads-EPS chain in action; leaders (Laurus 650→3,600 pre-split-era numbers) keep leading while everyone calls them "already run" |
| **MCX** | Long-time runner kept running (gold/crude derivative volume explosion) | "Already run" stocks keep running; the laggard-in-the-theme trap is real |

## Climactic tops & distribution

| Case | Setup | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **IRCTC / DMart / IEX — 18 October 2021** | Vertical final legs; all topped the same day on the broad-market shock; IRCTC-style one-bar reversals | Climactic tops reverse violently on the first external shock; vertical = everyone has bought = the last buyer is the market |
| **Tejas Networks** | BSNL 4G supply ran Mar-2023→Mar-2025 (~200%+ margins on the flagship product); order book ₹11,000cr → ₹1,200–1,300cr; next quarters printed −80/−90% de-growth and a ~₹200cr loss | High-base effect: order-book math alone can kill the chart; the wagon-count example in stage-analysis.md; internal story = slow distribution, and this one had a *fast* ending |
| **Trent / DMart growth-slowdown quarters** | First slow-growth quarter (LFL decay) after years of 30–40% growth | Peter Lynch's first-slow-quarter warning; stage 3 forms; the "value buy" chorus during de-growth is the tell |
| **A contraceptive company at ~240x P/E** *(name as heard)* | PAT inflated for 3–4 years, cash flow negative, stock ran anyway | Strong chart ≠ strong business; fundamentals veto pumped narratives; vertical rise + no cash conversion = fraud-shaped |

## Exits & risk

| Case | Setup | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Tata Elxsi** | Bought ~₹900; crowd called "overvalued" at 3,000/4,000/5,000; peaked ~11,000 on a 100x P/E; trailing stop captured the trend | Trailing stops answer the unanswerable; "how much do you love me?" has no number; same stock is a great hold for one person and a bad entry for another — position context changes the trade |
| **Apar Industries** | Exited ~4,500–5,000cr mcap (bought ~1,000–1,500cr) as it ran to ~14,000cr; Jan-2023 shakeout 1,800→1,400 in 3 weeks *without* a true 40-week-MA break, then ~1,400→~10,000 | Investors give back some top gain — part of the system; weekly-chart discipline prevents daily-chart shakeouts; don't dump whole positions when a new leg starts after deep corrections |
| **Alkyl Amines** | Exited far too early (4,500–5,000cr exit vs 14,000cr peak) | Early exits happen; the cure is not re-entry desperation but letting trailing stops run the *next* winner |
| **The DHFL uncle** *(as told on the show)* | Bought 350–400 → averaged down to avg ~70–75 → 70% of portfolio in one name → exited 40–45 | The full bias autopsy: loss aversion + averaging down + anchoring + sunk cost + mental accounting; the journaling habit exists to catch exactly this |
| **Fedbank Financial / gold-loan cluster (reprise)** | Bought the trigger day after weeks of visible consolidation | "Keep them on the radar, buy the technical trigger" — preparation converts scanning into execution without FOMO |

## Catalysts (business-side)

| Case | Catalyst type | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Deepak Fertilizer (ammonia backward integration, ~2021–22)** | Cost reduction via integration | Backward integration = margin lever visible in the P&L ladder |
| **Shriram Finance + MUFG stake** | Change of management/ownership | A big balance sheet improves credit rating + financing costs — an owner-change catalyst |
| **Jaiswal Neco** *(name as heard)* | Interest cost 18.5%→12.5% via refinancing; pledge removal as the balance sheet healed | De-leveraging catalysts show up in credit-rating reports before they show up in price narratives |
| **Bharat Wire Ropes (2017–18)** | Lender-led restructuring (debt→equity, interest forgiveness) as the cycle turned | Restructuring + cycle recovery = interest-cost collapse → PAT inflection |
| **Usha Martin** | Sold the steel business to Tata; focused on the core | Selling the wrong business is a catalyst; focus re-rates |
| **Windlas Biotech (2021–22)** | Promoter-announced buyback at the bottom (promoter didn't participate; holding % rose) | Buybacks as EPS math (₹100 profit ÷ shrinking share count); capital-allocation-literate promoters get re-rated when good times return |
| **GPIL — Ari Dongri mine approval talk (2 MT → 6 MT)** *(name as heard)* | Regulatory approval | In regulated industries, approvals are binary capacity catalysts |
| **HBL (Kavach), solar-pump order books** | Order-book wins | One buyer's decade of orders = supplier's decade of growth |
| **Intel — US government stake (~10%)** | Regulatory/strategic change | Policy shifts reprice incumbents overnight |
| **Thangamayil Jewellery → Chennai expansion** | Geographic expansion | New territory = new S-curve; the stock's move followed the store map |
| **New norms: truck AC cabins, ABS on bikes, EV instrument clusters (Pricol/Minda-type), cover glass, three-screen dashboards** | Regulatory change / new norms | Mandates create new addressable markets for 1–2 qualified suppliers — the "new normal" catalyst |
| **Mahindra (EV XUV 9e etc.; SUV share ~35%)** | New brands + market-share capture | Chart = reflection of the ground game (900→3,600 off the 2022 long-base breakout) |
| **Sansera — aerospace/defense/semiconductor line <₹50cr once** | Cash-cow funds a cash engine | Watch the *rate of change* of the new engine, not its current size |
| **Time Technoplast; Garware (again)** | Value-added transition | Commodity → value-added is a repeatable margin-expansion script |
| **CCL Products; Manorama** | Capex commercialized → utilization rising | Capex execution is a staged catalyst: announce → build → commission → utilization → margin |
| **Edelweiss → Nuvama (₹89,000cr parent vs ₹26,000cr demerged arm)** | Demerger/value unlocking | Spin-offs let each business get its own multiple; read the scheme with AI help |
| **Lemon Tree → asset-heavy unit demerger talk (Fleur Hotels)** *(as heard)* | Demerger | Asset-light management business separated from asset-heavy SPVs — PE re-rating mechanics simplified |
| **Devyani + Sapphire merger talk** *(as heard)* | M&A | Consolidation within Yum! franchisees; announcements create re-rating windows |
| **Vedanta — five-way demerger talk** | Value unlocking | Conglomerate splits are a standing Indian-market catalyst class |
| **Shrimp cycle (2016–19): Avanti Feeds et al.** | Demand-supply imbalance → decay | 30–35% CAGR for 5 years, then Ecuador/Vietnam supply returned, IPOs listed at 40x, charts topped in 2016–17 and never reclaimed; imbalance stories are trades, not religions |
| **Global Spirits (ethanol turnaround)** *(as heard)* | Turnaround | Result-season scan surfaces turnarounds early; knowing is different from buying — study feed |
| **Memory chips (SK Hynix/Micron/Samsung theme)** | Demand-supply imbalance, current cycle | The same playbook running live; trailing stops are how you participate in "bubbles" and exit the first crack |
| **Sandesh Ltd (Gujarati regional newspaper)** *(as heard)* | Secular decline | Consumption-pattern shifts kill categories; the chart said it for a decade |
| **Companies that become verbs (Zomato-it, Paytm-it, Xerox)** | Adoption language | Cultural verbs = saturation + climax risk in the chart |

## Reading list (from the episode)

**Technical non-negotiables ("the NCRT of the market"):**
1. **Nicholas Darvas — *How I Made $2,000,000 in the Stock Market*** (read multiple times; his third book: *You Can Still Make It in the Market*)
2. **William O'Neil — *How to Make Money in Stocks*** — chapters 1–9 matter most; ch.1 = 100 super-performers' charts; ch.2 = how to read charts
3. **Mark Minervini — *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard*** and ***Think & Trade Like a Champion*** (p.47: losses work geometrically against you)
4. **Stan Weinstein — *Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets*** (stage analysis)

**Fundamentals & business:**
5. **Peter Lynch — *One Up on Wall Street*** (the first-slow-growth-quarter warning; every stock is a business)
6. **Rajsekhara Iyer's chapter in *Masterclass with Super-Investors*** — combining technicals + fundamentals in practice
7. **Pat Dorsey — *The Little Book That Builds Wealth*** (moats: scale, network effects, switching costs) + ***Five Rules for Successful Stock Market Investing***
8. **John Magreta — *Understanding Michael Porter*** (the accessible version of five-forces)
9. **Santosh Nair — *Bulls, Bears and Other Beasts*** (Indian-market cycle history)
10. **Benjamin Graham — *The Intelligent Investor*** — ch.2 and ch.8 remain the relevant parts today
11. **Daniel Kahneman — *Thinking Fast and Slow*** (bias ground-work)
12. **Rolf Dobelli — *The Art of Thinking Clearly*** (two pages per bias — the journaling companion)
13. **Mark Andrew Lim — *Handbook of Technical Analysis*** (investor-psychology chapter cited on the show)

**Free resources named:**

- **SOIC** channel videos — especially the sector videos (one shipping-sector video covered 10 names)
- **CFA Society** channels
- **The Chartist** (Himanshu Sharma on X/Twitter) — his newsletter "chronicles" (129+ editions, 2–3 a week, described as a "Mr. Market sense-check") and his courses
